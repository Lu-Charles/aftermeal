# Research informing the next Aftermeal experiment

This is an engineering comparison, not a manuscript or an exhaustive literature review. Reported results below belong to different datasets and tasks; they cannot be compared directly with Aftermeal's group-balanced remaining-fraction error.

| Work | What the researchers did | Relevance and limits for Aftermeal |
|---|---|---|
| [Nutrition5k, CVPR 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Thames_Nutrition5k_Towards_Automatic_Nutritional_Understanding_of_Generic_Food_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf) | Used overhead RGB-D, food segmentation and calibrated capture geometry to derive a volume estimate, then learned mass from RGB plus volume. Table 4 reports 38.1 g MAE for image-only and 29.4 g with volume. | Test geometry as an additional signal, not just mask area. This is total dish mass, not a before/after leftover ratio; their depth was measured by a sensor. |
| [DPF-Nutrition, Foods 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10706621/) | Predicted depth with DPT and fused RGB/depth features using multiscale processing and cross-modal attention. Its Table 7 ablation reports mass MAE of 27.2 g for the RGB stream and 21.2 g for the full model. | Predicted depth is a testable option when users lack depth hardware. Its learned fusion and food-mass supervision matter; adding a generic depth visualization is not equivalent to reproducing the method. |
| [Food Portion Estimation via 3D Object Scaling, CVPR Workshops 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12257) | Used physical scene references, camera/object pose and known 3D food models to estimate volume from a 2D image. | Establish scale and geometry. Dependence on suitable 3D food models limits direct application to mixed dishes, smears and arbitrary leftovers. |
| [Food Waste Detection in Canteen Plates Using YOLOv11, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/13/7137) | Trained instance segmentation in a controlled canteen setting, separating plate/item regions and using area-based waste calculations. Reports mAP 0.343, precision 0.62 and recall 0.322 in its conclusion. | Plate localization and item-specific masks are useful ideas. Detection/segmentation metrics do not demonstrate accurate leftover mass; changing to YOLO alone is not an evidenced fix. |
| [FLIC, Applied Sciences 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/11/5465) | Paired full/leftover images, pixel annotations, physically measured masses and fixed camera geometry. Uses digital density to relate apparent area to mass. Includes residue and inedible remnants in its stated leftover definition. | Match the capture protocol, target definition and annotation quality. A residue label must not silently imply exactly zero mass. Dataset access remains unresolved in this project; no new access was obtained here. |

The [official Nutrition5k repository](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/Nutrition5k) supplies RGB/depth data and ingredient/dish masses, allows individual file downloads, and keeps incremental scans of a plate in the same split. Its full archive is 181.4 GB, so any feasibility experiment should start with a bounded subset rather than the full download. It is not a post-consumption paired dataset. Availability of data does not establish that an arbitrary third-party checkpoint reproduces the paper.

## What this changes in our approach

The current system has two distinct problems: deciding which visible material is a remaining portion, and estimating mass from shape/appearance. Our frozen binary food segmenter conflates some thin residue with food. The regression model uses global image embeddings and has no explicit geometric height measurement. These mechanisms are plausible contributors, not a complete causal explanation for every bad prediction.

1. **Evaluate residue recognition separately.** Define visible portion, thin residue, clean plate, inedible material and uncertain cases. Obtain independent labels for both before and after images. Measure confusion between residue and small genuine portions; do not substitute weight-zero labels for this visual task. AI annotations may seed training proposals but not certify the evaluation set.
2. **Compare appearance, area and geometry under the same split.** A small RGB-D dataset experiment can test the value of measured depth; an RGB-plus-predicted-depth experiment tests a different, more convenient hypothesis. Keep these results separate. For paired capture, compare height relative to the plate surface under controlled geometry, rather than treating raw depth brightness as volume.
3. **Retain density and food-type uncertainty.** Equal volume does not ensure equal mass, particularly across mixtures. A before/after ratio may reduce some scale/density effects under consistent capture, but that must be validated rather than assumed.
4. **Confirm on new servings.** Separate serving/session groups and all related stages of a meal. Report overall mass-fraction error, residue/small-portion failures and abstention coverage. Do not claim success from a corrected gallery image or repeat tuning on the same inspected test records.

These are proposed experiments, not implemented improvements. No cited model was downloaded or benchmarked as part of this literature review. Some publisher full-text requests failed; FLIC/YOLO and the Nutrition5k table were also checked through indexed primary-source passages. The linked papers establish the methods described, not expected accuracy for Aftermeal.
